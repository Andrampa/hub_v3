# DIEM product launch and communication strategy

## Purpose

DIEM will launch three connected public products before the end of the year:
the new DIEM Hub, the new Household Survey Explorer and EVE 2.0. The campaign
will present them as a renewed DIEM evidence offer while giving each audience a
clear reason to use the product most relevant to its work.

The Hub is the public front door for discovering DIEM evidence. The Household
Survey Explorer supports analysis of household monitoring results. EVE 2.0
brings together better flood data, relevant data from other FAO teams, VISTA,
Floodex and new field data collection and impact-assessment capabilities.
VISTA will be launched as part of EVE 2.0, not as a separate product.

## Objectives

- Introduce the new Hub as the main entry point to DIEM evidence.
- Replace the legacy Hub and move users from the former Monitoring System app
  and old dashboards to the new Household Survey Explorer.
- Build awareness and practical use of EVE 2.0 and its expanded flood evidence
  and field assessment offer.
- Explain which products are relevant to each country and audience.
- Turn awareness into use through country outreach, training and continuing
  support for all DIEM countries.

## Audiences and messages

| Audience | Priority message and action |
|---|---|
| FAO and OER colleagues | Understand the three-product offer and direct users to the appropriate product. |
| All FAO Representatives | Share the new Hub with relevant national counterparts. |
| Countries with active monitoring systems | Promote the Hub and Household Survey Explorer and nominate users for training. |
| Countries exposed to flood hazards | Promote EVE 2.0 and identify operational users for training. |
| Governments, partners, researchers and donors | Find, use and share authoritative DIEM evidence through the relevant product. |

**DIEM Hub.** A modern front door for discovering DIEM evidence by country,
product and analytical pathway, with direct connections to household surveys,
hazard impacts, flood services and data access.

**Household Survey Explorer.** A clearer way to explore household monitoring
results across countries, survey rounds and themes, with analysis, export and
sharing functions in one experience.

**EVE 2.0.** A stronger flood evidence and impact-assessment platform,
combining better data, relevant data from other FAO teams, VISTA and Floodex
with field data collection and validation capabilities.

## Launch sequence

| Milestone | Main activity |
|---|---|
| T0 Align | Confirm the shared narrative, final product descriptions, owners, audiences and required communication assets. |
| T1 Team review | Present the new Hub to Team 1A and allow one working day to incorporate relevant comments. |
| T2 Clearance and preparation | Ask OCC for rapid clearance of the Hub, supported by a concise old-versus-new comparison. Prepare communication materials, country lists and the OER event in parallel. |
| T3 Quiet publication | After OCC clearance, publish the Hub and replace the legacy Hub. Replace the former Monitoring System app and old dashboards with the Household Survey Explorer as soon as possible. |
| T4 Internal launch | Hold a one-hour OER event and activate FAO screens, the intranet and the FAO latest mailing list. |
| T5 External campaign | Run coordinated external communication for the Hub and Explorer, and for EVE 2.0 including VISTA. Use DIEM, OCC, ESS and partner channels. |
| T6 Adoption and support | Deliver targeted training, support champion countries intensively and provide lighter online support and guidance to all DIEM countries. |

All milestones should happen before the end of the year. Preparation can run in
parallel so that communication follows soon after quiet publication.

## Communication activities

### Internal communication

Communication colleagues should prepare two packages: one for the Hub and
Household Survey Explorer, and one for EVE 2.0. These should be adapted for FAO
screens, the intranet and the FAO latest mailing list. The DIEM newsletter
should announce all three products to the DIEM community.

Rein should be asked to convene a one-hour OER event. The event should briefly
show the Hub, the Household Survey Explorer and EVE 2.0 through a connected
country journey, then explain country outreach, training and support.

### External communication

Communication colleagues should support two coordinated campaigns:

- The Hub and Household Survey Explorer, aimed at broad DIEM evidence and
  household monitoring audiences.
- EVE 2.0, including VISTA, aimed at flood, emergency, early warning, GIS and
  impact-assessment audiences.

Sophie and ESS have offered to promote DIEM through the monthly Statistics
newsletter, a banner on the FAO Statistics website when supported by an OCC
news or PR article, and amplification through FAO Statistics social channels
and the Global Network of Data Officers and Statisticians on LinkedIn.

All FAO Representatives should receive a ready-to-send Hub message. The
Household Survey Explorer should be added for countries with active monitoring
systems, while EVE 2.0 should be added for countries with flood hazards.
Country-specific links should be included wherever possible.

## Communication materials

- One short narrative and approved description for each product.
- Two communication packs: Hub plus Explorer, and EVE 2.0 including VISTA.
- OCC news or web copy and DIEM and Statistics newsletter copy.
- Social cards, intranet and screen graphics, and short demonstrations.
- OER invitation, agenda and demonstration script.
- Ready-to-send country email templates and relevant direct links.
- A training-interest form and list of country focal points.

## Training and country support

Formal Hub training is unlikely to be necessary; short guidance should be
sufficient. Training is needed for EVE 2.0, including risk and field data
functions, and for the Household Survey Explorer.

Countries should be associated with one or both training streams according to
their monitoring activity, flood exposure, operational need, skills and
interest. If funding is available, Andrea or regional advisers could deliver
intensive two- to three-day missions in one or two champion countries. If not,
Andrea could deliver a two-day online training of trainers, with one day for
EVE 2.0 and one for the Household Survey Explorer.

Champion countries should receive deeper follow-up, but support should not stop
there. All DIEM countries should have access to an appropriate level of support,
such as recorded demonstrations, short online orientations, office hours,
guidance material or regional sessions.

## Coordination and results

The DIEM product team should own the narrative, technical accuracy, country
targeting and demonstrations. Team 1A provides the first Hub review; OCC clears
the Hub and supports external communication; communication colleagues prepare
channel-ready assets; Sophie and ESS amplify through Statistics channels; Rein
convenes the OER event; FAO Representatives support country dissemination; and
Andrea and regional advisers lead training and country support.

A small coordination group should maintain one milestone tracker, approved
copy set, asset list, country list and distribution calendar. Results should be
reviewed through visits and repeat use, movement from the Hub into the Explorer
and EVE, downloads and shared links, event and newsletter engagement, training
participation, countries reached, and practical examples of use.
